## swallowing-and-tube-feeding
- Swallowing problems (dysphagia) are part of late-stage HD and can lead to choking, recurrent aspiration pneumonia, weight loss, and fear at mealtimes. [s35]
- Weight loss is almost universal in late-stage HD. People with late-stage HD usually die from medical complications of severe neurological disability. [s35]
- A speech-language pathologist can assess which textures are safest; a dietitian can advise on calories and supplements. [s35]
- Some people with HD want a gastrostomy tube if they lose weight or choke; others prefer not to have one. Ideally this is decided, and written down, earlier in the illness. [s35]
- In advanced dementia, careful hand feeding has been shown to be as good as tube feeding for death, aspiration pneumonia, function and comfort; tube feeding is linked with agitation, restraints and complications. This evidence is from dementia, not HD. [s33]
- Tube feeding is a medical treatment that a substitute decision maker can accept or decline, guided by the person's wishes. [s33]

## deciding-for-someone
- If the person has not expressed a view about a feeding tube and can no longer communicate, the family is left to decide on their behalf. [s35]
- In British Columbia, if no representative has been named, the health care provider chooses a temporary substitute decision maker in an order set by law, starting with the spouse, then adult children. [s32]
- A substitute decision maker should decide as the person would have wanted, based on their known wishes and values. [s32, s33]
- Involve the person as far as possible: some people with late-stage HD can still show agreement or displeasure, even with very little speech. [s35]
- Offering a recommendation based on the person's wishes can lift some of the weight from the family. [?]

## family-meetings
- Use a structured approach: set up the meeting, find out what people understand, give information in plain words, respond to emotion, and agree a plan. [s20]
- Include everyone who should be there, including family on the phone. [?]
- Disagreement often reflects grief, guilt or fear. Explore what lies behind each view before trying to resolve it. [?]

## care-setting-and-carers
- People in the late stages of HD need 24-hour supervision and help. This often leads to carer burnout, and most people in late-stage HD move to care outside the home. [s35]
- An older person whose spouse is retired can sometimes stay at home with in-home personal care. [s35]
- In BC, long-term care is arranged through the health authority's home and community care services, after an assessment. [s34]
- Consider hospice or palliative care when the person is mute, unable to walk, losing weight or refusing food. [s35]
- Many people with HD ask the team not to perform resuscitation or intubation at a certain point in the illness. Wishes should be documented. [s35]
- Carers need support for themselves; ask how they are coping. [s35]

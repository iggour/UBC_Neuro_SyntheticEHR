# When your child may have juvenile Huntington disease
## Appointment preparation sheet (for parents and carers)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-29. Reviewed by: PENDING (project clinician)
Educational only. Not medical advice. Draft for Group A, not yet clinically reviewed.

### Things worth telling the team
- Changes at school: grades, concentration, handwriting, or comments from teachers.
- Changes in movement: stiffness, clumsiness, falls, or speech becoming slower or slurred.
- Changes in mood or behaviour, even if you thought they were "just being a teenager".
- Any illness in grandparents or other relatives, even if it had a different name, such as "dementia" or "Parkinson's".

### At the appointment
- Bring someone with you if you can, and a notebook.
- It is fine to ask the team to slow down, or to say the same thing again.
- You do not have to decide everything today.

### Looking after yourselves
- Shock, anger, sadness and guilt are all common. None of this is your fault.
- Ask about support for parents and for brothers and sisters.

### Support
- Huntington Society of Canada: https://www.huntingtonsociety.ca/
- HDYO, information for young people and families: https://www.hdyo.org/
- Juvenile HD chapter, Huntington Society of Canada Physician's Guide: https://hsc.huntingtonsociety.ca/public/resources/physicians-guide.pdf

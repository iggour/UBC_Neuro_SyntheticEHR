# When you are worried about the mood of someone with Huntington disease
## Appointment preparation sheet (for family members and carers)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-23. Reviewed by: project clinician (sources and content checked 2026-10-26)
Educational only. Not medical advice. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

### Things worth telling the team
- Changes in mood, sleep, appetite or interest in things they used to enjoy.
- More irritability or outbursts than usual.
- Anything they have said about being a burden or not wanting to be here, even if it sounded like a joke.
- Recent changes: stopping work or driving, family news, a new medication.

### Tick the questions you want to ask (question builder)
- [ ] Could any of their medications be affecting their mood?
- [ ] Is this depression, and can it be treated?
- [ ] Can you see them alone for part of the appointment?
- [ ] What should I do if they say something worrying at home?
- [ ] Should I keep their medications, and how?
- [ ] Who can we call out of hours?
- [ ] What support is there for me as a carer?

### At the appointment
- It is fine to ask the team to see your family member alone. People are sometimes more open without family in the room.
- Bring their medication list, or the boxes.
- Write down what is agreed, including who to call.

### Looking after yourself
- Caring for someone with HD is hard, and it is normal to feel tired, sad or angry.
- You do not have to do this alone. Ask the team about carer support.

### Support
- Huntington Society of Canada: https://www.huntingtonsociety.ca/
- If you or your family member have thoughts of not wanting to live: call or text 9-8-8 (Canada), any time.
- In an emergency: call 911.

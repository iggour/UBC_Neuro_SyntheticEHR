# When your teenager has juvenile Huntington disease and seizures
## Appointment preparation sheet (for parents and carers)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-29. Reviewed by: PENDING (project clinician)
Educational only. Not medical advice. Draft for Group A, not yet clinically reviewed. If a seizure lasts more than five minutes, call 911.

### Things worth telling the team
- Every seizure: the date, what happened, how long it lasted, and what your child was doing before.
- Any missed or late doses of medicine, or side effects such as tiredness. It helps to know, not to blame.
- How school is going: grades, tiredness, friends, and anything teachers have said.
- Changes in mood, sleep or appetite.

### At the appointment
- It is routine for teenagers to see the team alone for part of the visit. This helps them speak openly.
- Bring the seizure diary and the medicine box.
- Let your teenager answer first; you will be asked for your view too.

### Keeping safe and keeping independence
- Showers are safer than baths, and the bathroom door should not be locked.
- Swimming is safer with a lifeguard or a supervising adult who knows about the seizures, and never alone.
- Talk with your teenager about how to keep these rules while respecting their privacy.

## delivering-a-result
- Give the result in person, never by phone or mail. The person can decide not to be told, even on the day. [s6]
- Check readiness, give a short warning, then say the result in plain words. [s9]
- After the news, allow silence and respond to emotion before giving more information. [s9]
- Check what has been understood, and offer a written summary and another appointment. [s9]

## what-a-positive-result-means
- It shows the gene change was inherited. It is not a diagnosis of the illness; the person may be well for many years. [s6]
- 40 or more CAG repeats is the full-penetrance range. [s1]
- Only limited information can be given about age at onset, type of symptoms, severity or speed of progression. [s6]

## family-risk
- Each child of an affected parent has an independent 50% chance of inheriting the gene change. One sibling's result does not change another sibling's risk. [s3]
- Whether and when to tell relatives is the person's own decision. Do not pass results to others without explicit permission. [s6]

## after-the-result
- Contact the person within a week of the result, whatever it is. Start follow-up yourself if there has been no contact after a month. [s6]
- Post-test counselling should not depend on ability to pay. [s6]
- A positive result is a period of higher risk of distress. Ask directly about thoughts of not wanting to live, and confirm support for the coming days. [s8]
- Reproductive options can be discussed later: going ahead without testing, prenatal diagnosis, preimplantation genetic diagnosis, donor insemination, adoption. [s6]

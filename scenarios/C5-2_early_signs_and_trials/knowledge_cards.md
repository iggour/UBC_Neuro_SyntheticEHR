## when-hd-starts
- "Manifest HD" has traditionally been diagnosed on motor signs: a clinician is 99% confident (diagnostic confidence level 4) of an otherwise unexplained movement disorder in a person with the gene expansion or a family history. [s41]
- An MDS task force proposed also counting cognitive signs (minor or major neurocognitive disorder), and described a "prodromal" period of subtle changes before manifest HD. [s41]
- Information from family or co-workers can help show cognitive change over time. [s41]
- In early-stage HD, people are largely functional and may continue to work, drive, handle money and live independently. [s36]
- Losing the ability to do one's usual job is usually the first functional loss; changes in role can help people keep working. [s36]
- The time around diagnosis is a critical period for suicide risk in HD. Ask directly; asking does not increase suicidal thoughts. [s8, s12]

## trials-and-research
- As of 2026-10-29, no treatment had been approved anywhere to slow the progression of HD; several were in clinical trials (checked 2026-10-29). [s43]
- As of 2026-10-29, examples included: votoplam (a Phase 3 trial, INVEST-HD, opened in 2026); AMT-130, a gene therapy given by brain surgery, under discussion with regulators after early-phase studies; and pridopidine, which was not approved in Europe and is being studied in a new Phase 3 trial. Roche's tominersen Phase 2 trial (GENERATION HD2) was reported as discontinued (checked 2026-10-29). [s43]
- Trials have eligibility criteria (for example disease stage, age, other health), often include a placebo or comparison group, and many sites are outside Canada. [?]
- Enroll-HD is a worldwide observational study and research platform for HD families: participants contribute information over time; it does not provide treatment (checked 2026-10-29). [s42]
- Large observational studies have expanded research opportunities for people at risk of HD and with HD. [s35]
- To find trials: ClinicalTrials.gov, HDTrialFinder (https://hdtrialfinder.net/), the Huntington Society of Canada, and the HD clinic. [s43, s35]
- Treatments sold for a fee outside regulated trials (for example "stem cell therapy" clinics) are unproven, can cause harm and debt, and may make a person ineligible for genuine trials. [?]

## safety-at-home
- First-line support follows LIVES: Listen; Inquire about needs and concerns; Validate; Enhance safety; Support. [s37]
- Ask about violence in private, explain confidentiality and its limits, and do not pressure the person to disclose or to leave. [s37]
- Assess immediate risk, for example: is the violence getting worse; are there weapons; has the partner threatened to kill; is she afraid to go home. [s37]
- Outbursts in HD can become violent. Safety comes first, and carers should have an exit strategy for leaving the house or calling the police if an outburst threatens to get out of control. [s36]
- Ask about firearms. Suicide is common in HD; ask directly about suicidal thoughts and access to means. [s36, s12]
- VictimLinkBC gives 24-hour help to people affected by family violence in BC: call or text 1-800-563-0808. In an emergency, call 911. [s38]
- Document carefully, and agree a safe way to contact the person. [s37]

## irritability-and-carers
- Irritability and temper outbursts are among the most common complaints from HD families. They may come from loss of impulse control, feeling overwhelmed, or frustration, and can be a symptom of depression. [s36]
- The person may recover quickly and forget an outburst, leaving the family shaken. [s36]
- Strategies for carers: learn the warning signs, avoid known triggers, stay calm, avoid confrontations and ultimatums, and leave the area if an outburst threatens to become violent. [s36]
- Treatment of irritability (and any depression) can help; review with the person's HD team. [s36]
- Families may confront someone who looks like the person they knew but shows callous behaviour and poor judgement. Carers should be encouraged to talk to the physician about their own frustrations. [s36]
- Point carers to family support, such as the Huntington Society of Canada: https://www.huntingtonsociety.ca/

## testing-a-teenager
- Predictive testing is reserved for adults who have had a careful discussion with a genetic counsellor about their risks and the benefits and harms of the test. [s35]
- HD predictive testing guidelines do not recommend predictive testing under 18. [s6]
- For conditions that typically start in adulthood, predictive testing should be deferred until the young person can decide for themselves. [s21]
- A young person can meet a genetic counsellor to talk about their risk and choices before deciding anything; predictive testing protocols include time to reflect. [s6]
- Teenagers in HD families may be affected by stress at home; offer support for them as well (for example HDYO, https://www.hdyo.org/). [?]

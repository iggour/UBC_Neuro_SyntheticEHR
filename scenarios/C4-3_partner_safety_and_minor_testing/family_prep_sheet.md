# When caring for someone with HD feels unsafe
## Preparation sheet (for partners and family members)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-29. Reviewed by: project owner (checked 2026-10-30)
Educational only. Not medical advice. If you are in immediate danger, call 911.

### Things worth telling your doctor
- What happens during outbursts, how often, and whether they are getting worse.
- Whether you have been hurt, threatened, or are afraid to go home.
- Whether there are firearms or other weapons in the house.
- Anything the person has said about not wanting to live.
- How you and your children are coping.

### A simple safety plan
- Know the warning signs and leave the room early if things escalate.
- Keep your phone, keys and important papers where you can grab them.
- Agree with a friend or relative where you can go, day or night.
- Ask for help to get firearms out of the house.

### Support
- VictimLinkBC, 24 hours, call or text 1-800-563-0808: https://victimlinkbc.ca/
- Huntington Society of Canada (family support): https://www.huntingtonsociety.ca/
- HDYO, information for young people in HD families: https://www.hdyo.org/
- Huntington Society of Canada, Physician's Guide (irritability and outbursts, chapter 5): https://hsc.huntingtonsociety.ca/public/resources/physicians-guide.pdf
- WHO clinical handbook on responding to partner violence: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-RHR-14.26
- Suicide crisis line, call or text 9-8-8. In an emergency: call 911.

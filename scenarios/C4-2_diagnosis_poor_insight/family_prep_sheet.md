# When HD is diagnosed but the person does not see the changes
## Appointment preparation sheet (for partners and family members)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-29. Reviewed by: project owner (checked 2026-10-30)
Educational only. Not medical advice. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.

### Things worth knowing
- Many people with HD do not notice their own changes. This "unawareness" is often part of the illness, not stubbornness or denial.
- Arguing about the diagnosis rarely helps. Talking about specific problems (sleep, tiredness, work) often goes better.

### Things worth telling the team
- What is happening at work: warnings, suspensions, plans to resign.
- Any changes with money: unusual spending, debts, missed bills.
- Outbursts at home, and whether there are firearms in the house.
- What your children know and have asked.

### Tick the questions you want to ask (question builder)
- [ ] What does this diagnosis mean for us now?
- [ ] What should happen with work? Should anyone resign?
- [ ] Can we see a social worker about work, disability benefits and money?
- [ ] How can we protect our finances?
- [ ] What legal papers should we think about now (power of attorney, representation agreement)?
- [ ] What does this mean for our daughter or son, and what should we tell them?
- [ ] What help is there for irritability and sleep?

### At the appointment
- It is fine to ask to speak with the team alone for a few minutes.
- Do not make big decisions about work or money on the day of the diagnosis.

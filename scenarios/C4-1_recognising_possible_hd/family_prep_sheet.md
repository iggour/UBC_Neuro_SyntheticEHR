# When someone you love "hasn't been themselves"
## Appointment preparation sheet (for partners and family members)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-29. Reviewed by: PENDING (project clinician)
Educational only. Not medical advice. Draft, not yet clinically reviewed. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911.

### Things worth telling the doctor
- Changes in temper, patience or behaviour, with examples and dates.
- Problems at work: missed deadlines, conflicts, warnings.
- Changes in sleep, mood, drinking or spending.
- Small movements you have noticed: fidgeting, dropping things, twitching, stumbling.
- What is known, or not known, about the family history (for example if they were adopted).

### Tick the questions you want to ask (question builder)
- [ ] Could these changes have a medical cause?
- [ ] What tests or referrals are needed?
- [ ] Can I speak with you alone for a few minutes?
- [ ] What should we do if there is an outburst at home?
- [ ] Should they keep working while this is checked?
- [ ] Who can we call before the next appointment?

### At the appointment
- The person may not notice the changes themselves. This can be part of an illness, not stubbornness.
- It is fine to ask to speak with the doctor privately.
- Bring a short list of examples rather than trying to remember everything.

### Your safety
- If outbursts are frightening, plan how you would leave the house or call for help.
- Ask about safe storage or removal of firearms.

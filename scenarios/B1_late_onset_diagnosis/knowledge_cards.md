## late-onset-hd
- Late-onset HD usually means onset after age 60. Among Caucasian HD cohorts, about 4 to 12% of people have late onset. [s27]
- The diagnosis can be missed in older people because HD seems unlikely at that age. [s27]
- Most people with late-onset HD have 44 or fewer CAG repeats. [s27]
- Chorea is the most common first sign, but gait unsteadiness and falls are prominent, and cognitive problems may be the main cause of disability. [s27]
- Progression may be slower than in typical adult-onset HD. [s27]
- A gene test confirms whether the HD expansion is present; it is the clinician who judges that symptoms of HD are present. [s35]

## giving-an-unexpected-diagnosis
- SPIKES: Setting; Perception; Invitation; Knowledge (warning shot, plain words, small amounts); Emotions; Strategy and summary. [s20]
- When the patient expects "nothing", check expectations first and give a clear warning before the result. [s20]
- Gene testing should be used routinely when someone appears to have HD but has no known family history. [s35]
- About 5% of people who develop HD know of no family history. In a British Columbia study, almost a quarter of newly diagnosed patients had no family history of HD. [s35, s30]
- Possible reasons: a parent who died before symptoms were recognised, a past diagnosis under another name, adoption or misattributed parentage, or a new expansion from a parent's high-normal repeat. [s35]
- Careful counselling is needed, because this may be the first diagnosis of HD in the family. [s27]
- Start planning ahead early: the person's wishes are easier to record while they can still express complex ideas. [s35]

## risk-for-the-family
- Each child of a person with an HD gene expansion has a 50% chance of inheriting it. [s19]
- 36 to 39 repeats is the "reduced penetrance" range: not everyone with a repeat in this range develops symptoms in a normal lifespan. 40 or more repeats causes HD in a normal lifespan. [s35]
- Penetrance depends on repeat size and age, and estimates for 36-39 repeats are uncertain. Population studies suggest about 1 in 400 people carry 36 or more repeats, many without ever being diagnosed. [s28, s29]
- The repeat size can change when passed on; larger changes happen more often when the gene is passed on by a father. [s35]
- Avoid giving precise penetrance figures in a first conversation. Offer referral to a genetic counsellor with detailed knowledge of HD genetics. [s35]
- Whether to tell relatives, and whether to have a predictive test, is each person's own decision, made with counselling. [s6]
- Predictive testing of healthy children is generally deferred until they can decide for themselves. [s21]
- In Canada, no one can be required to take a genetic test or disclose a result as a condition of a contract or service, such as insurance. [s7]

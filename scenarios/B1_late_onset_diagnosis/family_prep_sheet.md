# When Huntington disease is diagnosed later in life
## Appointment preparation sheet (for the person and their family)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-29. Reviewed by: project owner (checked 2026-10-30)
Educational only. Not medical advice.

### Things worth telling the team
- Changes you have noticed: movements, balance and falls, memory and planning, mood.
- Anything about parents, brothers, sisters or other relatives: illnesses, how they died, and anything unusual in their later years, even if it had another name.
- What you already know or have read about HD.

### Tick the questions you want to ask (question builder)
- [ ] What does this diagnosis mean for me now?
- [ ] How quickly is it likely to change?
- [ ] How can I have HD when no one in my family did?
- [ ] What does my result mean for my children and grandchildren?
- [ ] How and when should I tell my family? Can someone help?
- [ ] Can my children see a genetic counsellor?
- [ ] Could a test result affect my family's insurance?
- [ ] What support is there, and who do we call with questions?

### At the appointment
- Bring someone with you, and a notebook.
- Ask the team to explain any numbers in plain words.
- You do not have to decide anything about your family today.

### Looking after yourselves
- Shock is normal, especially when no one expected it.
- Telling family is a personal decision. A genetic counsellor can help you plan what to say.

### Support
- Huntington Society of Canada: https://www.huntingtonsociety.ca/
- Huntington Society of Canada, Physician's Guide (genetics and planning ahead): https://hsc.huntingtonsociety.ca/public/resources/physicians-guide.pdf

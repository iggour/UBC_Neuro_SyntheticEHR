## what-the-gnda-covers
- Genetic Non-Discrimination Act (checked 2026-10-29; current to 2026-09-30): no one may require a person to take a genetic test, or to disclose genetic test results, as a condition of providing goods or services or entering or continuing a contract; and no one in those situations may collect, use or disclose a person's genetic test results without written consent. [s7]
- A "genetic test" means a test that analyses DNA, RNA or chromosomes, for example to predict disease or monitor, diagnose or give a prognosis. [s7]
- Health care practitioners providing care, and researchers with research participants, are exempt. Breaking the Act is an offence with large fines or imprisonment. [s7]
- The Act does not stop insurers using information about a person's diagnosed condition or symptoms, even if the condition is genetic. Family medical history is not protected. [s46]
- So a person with diagnosed HD should answer insurance health questions truthfully, and get independent advice before applying. [s46]
- Misrepresenting health information on an insurance application can lead to a claim being denied or a policy being cancelled. [?]

## work-and-disclosure
- The Canadian Human Rights Act lists "genetic characteristics" as a prohibited ground, and treats refusing a genetic test or disclosure as discrimination on that ground (checked 2026-10-29). [s44]
- The Canadian Human Rights Act applies to federally regulated employers, such as banks, airlines and telecommunications companies; most private employers, like a video game studio, are provincially regulated. [?]
- In BC, private employers fall under the BC Human Rights Code. It does not list genetic characteristics, but protects physical and mental disability, including a perceived disability; firing someone over a concern they might develop a disability is an example of discrimination (checked 2026-10-29). [s45]
- Employers have a duty to accommodate disability up to the point of undue hardship. [?]
- Losing one's usual job is often the first functional loss in HD; a change of role can help people keep working. Decisions about retiring or applying for disability should be made with social workers and physicians; know the employer's disability plan. [s36]
- Social workers can help with disability, insurance and financial planning. [s35]
- Disclosure at work is usually the person's choice. To ask for accommodation, they may share the functional needs (for example extra time or code-review support) rather than the diagnosis itself. [?]

# Thinking about a predictive test for Huntington disease
## Appointment preparation sheet (for people at risk and their families)
Drafted by: Claude (Anthropic), 2026-10-22. Reviewed by: [pending]
Educational only. Not medical advice. Your genetics team is your main source of information.

### What may happen at the first appointment
- You talk about why you are thinking about testing and what you already know.
- The team explains what the test can and cannot tell you.
- Usually there is no blood test that day. Most people are given at least a month to think before deciding.
- You can stop at any time, and you can choose not to hear a result.

### Tick the questions you want to ask (question builder)
- [ ] What could my result show, including "in-between" results?
- [ ] Can the test tell me when symptoms might start?
- [ ] How long does the whole process take?
- [ ] Who will give me the result, and can someone come with me?
- [ ] What support is there after the result, whatever it is?
- [ ] Do I have to tell my partner, family, employer or insurer?
- [ ] How could my result affect my children or plans for children?
- [ ] What if I change my mind?

### What you can bring
- A person you trust.
- A list of your questions.
- Any information you have about your family's diagnosis (for example a relative's test result).

### Support
- Huntington Society of Canada: information and family services (link to add).
- If you ever feel you can't cope or have thoughts of not wanting to live: call or text 9-8-8 (Canada), or call 911 in an emergency.

## pre-test-counselling-essentials
- The minimum age for predictive testing is 18. [s6]
- The decision belongs only to the person concerned. Family, partners, employers and insurers must not pressure them. [s6]
- Counselling is given by a trained multidisciplinary team, and consent is documented. [s6]
- Allow at least one month between pre-test information and the decision to be tested, unless there is an exceptional reason. [s6]
- Encourage the person to bring a companion. Plan follow-up support before and after the test. [s6]
- Results are given in person, never by phone or mail. The person can decide at any time not to be told. [s6]

## what-a-predictive-test-can-and-cannot-tell
- It shows whether the gene change was inherited. It does not make a clinical diagnosis. [s6]
- Only limited information can be given about age at onset, type of symptoms, severity or speed of progression. [s6]
- CAG ranges: 26 or fewer normal; 27 to 35 intermediate; 36 to 39 reduced penetrance; 40 or more full penetrance. [s1]
- Pre-test counselling should cover all possible outcomes, including intermediate and reduced-penetrance results. [s6]

## privacy-and-insurance
- Do not share test information with third parties without the person's explicit permission. [s6]
- In Canada, the Genetic Non-Discrimination Act restricts requiring a genetic test or its results as a condition of a contract or service. [s7, PENDING: check current wording before release]

## mood-and-safety
- Learning about genetic risk can be overwhelming. Ask about mood, coping and support. [s8, PENDING]
- Asking directly about thoughts of not wanting to live is safe and helps you plan support. [s8, PENDING]
- In Canada, anyone can call or text 9-8-8 for suicide crisis support.

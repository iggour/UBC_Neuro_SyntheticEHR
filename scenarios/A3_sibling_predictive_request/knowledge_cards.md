## testing-healthy-children
- For conditions that usually do not start until adulthood, predictive testing should be deferred until the child can decide whether to be tested. [s21]
- HD predictive testing guidelines do not recommend predictive testing for people under 18. [s6, s16]
- Reasons: no medical benefit in childhood; it protects the child's right to an open future and the right not to know; a result may change how the child is seen and raised. [s21, s26]
- Requests should be discussed thoroughly with the parents before any decision, rather than refused without discussion. [s26]
- When parents ask for testing of a healthy child with no medical or other benefit, clinicians are not obliged to test. They should explain their reasons clearly and document the discussion. [s21]
- If not testing might carry greater risk than testing, ask for advice from medical genetics or an ethicist. [s21]
- Direct-to-consumer genetic testing is not recommended for children. [s21]

## if-owen-changes
- Each child of a parent who carries the HD gene expansion has a 50% chance of inheriting it. [s19]
- A sibling of a child with JHD could also develop symptoms in childhood or adolescence, if they inherited the expansion. [?]
- When a young person from an HD family has clear neurological signs, a genetic test to confirm the diagnosis is not controversial. This is diagnostic, not predictive, testing. [s16]
- Changes in school performance or behaviour alone have many other causes; neurological review, and baseline assessments that can be repeated later, help tell them apart. [s16, s18]
- Parents can be asked to note changes and bring them to a review, rather than watching for signs every day. [?]

## talking-with-owen
- Children in HD families are affected by the illness of a parent or sibling, and family stress can affect their behaviour. [s18]
- Children and youth should be given information about genetic testing as they become able to understand it. [s21]
- Honest, simple answers are better than reassurance that may later turn out to be untrue. Parents can say "We don't know yet, and when you're older you can decide whether to find out." [?]
- HDYO has material for children and young people in HD families: https://www.hdyo.org/

## 1. Reason for referral

Two-year history of right hand tremor and slowness; assessment for Parkinson disease.

## 2. History of present illness

Mr. H. is a 61-year-old right-handed man who noticed a tremor of the right hand at rest about 2 years ago. Over the same period his handwriting has become smaller, his right hand is slower on the guitar, and he drags his right foot when tired. His wife says his right arm swings less when he walks. He has had constipation for about 3 years and has lost most of his sense of smell, "for years". For about 5 years he has acted out his dreams in sleep, punching and shouting; his wife has moved to another bed after being hit.

He denies falls, dizziness on standing, fainting, urinary urgency, swallowing difficulty, hallucinations, or memory complaints. No prior use of antipsychotics, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine or valproate. No head injury, stroke or encephalitis. No sudden onset; symptoms have progressed slowly and started on the right. He was referred by his family physician in November 2024; MRI brain was done on 2025-01-08 and DAT-SPECT on 2025-01-21.

## 3. Past medical history

- No diabetes, no hypertension, no heart disease, no depression or anxiety history

No dopaminergic or dopamine-blocking medications.

## 6. Allergies

## 7. Social history

Mr. H. is a high school music teacher and plans to retire in about 4 years. He lives in North Vancouver with his wife, and two adult children live nearby. He plays guitar in a community band and worries that his tremor will end this. His wife accompanies him and is very involved. He has extended health benefits through the school district. Alcohol 1-2 drinks per week; never smoked; coffee 2 cups per day; no recreational drug use. He drives without difficulty.

## 8. Family history

Mother had a "tremor" in her 70s that was never diagnosed. No known family history of Parkinson disease, dementia, or early-onset parkinsonism. Genetic testing is not indicated given late onset and minimal family history.

## 9. Physical examination

**Vitals:** Lying BP 128/78, HR 68; standing 1 minute 124/76, 3 minutes 122/76 (no orthostatic hypotension). Weight 78.5 kg, height 178 cm.
**Functional status:** independent, working full time.

**Neurological:**
- Mental status: alert, oriented, mild hypomimia, mild hypophonia; MoCA 27/30 (lost points on delayed recall).
- Cranial nerves: full vertical and horizontal eye movements with normal saccades; no gaze palsy; smell testing (UPSIT) 18/40, markedly reduced.
- Bradykinesia: right greater than left, with decrement in amplitude on finger tapping, hand opening/closing and foot tapping.
- Tremor: rest tremor of the right hand at 4-5 Hz, re-emergent on posture; none on the left.
- Rigidity: cogwheel rigidity at the right wrist, mild at the right elbow.
- Gait: reduced right arm swing, normal stride length, no freezing. Pull test: steady, recovers with one step.
- No pyramidal signs, no cerebellar signs, no apraxia, no autonomic findings. Reflexes symmetric.
**MDS-UPDRS Part III (OFF, untreated): 32. Hoehn and Yahr stage 2.**
**Acute levodopa challenge:** after levodopa/carbidopa 200/50 mg orally, MDS-UPDRS III 90 minutes later was 20 (37.5% improvement).
**Mood:** PHQ-9 = 5.

## 10. Investigations

**MRI brain (2025-01-08, 3.0T):** no midbrain atrophy (no hummingbird sign), no putaminal slit or hyperintense rim, no hot cross bun sign, no pontocerebellar atrophy, no normal-pressure hydrocephalus pattern. Punctate deep white matter foci (Fazekas 1). No infarcts or mass. No abnormal iron deposition.

**DAT-SPECT ([123I]ioflupane, 2025-01-21):** asymmetrically reduced striatal uptake, greater in the left putamen than the right, with relative preservation of the caudate nuclei.

**Laboratory (2025-01-20):** hemoglobin 148 g/L, TSH 1.9 mIU/L, B12 388 pmol/L, ferritin 120 ug/L, creatinine 82 umol/L, ALT 24 U/L, HbA1c 5.4%.

## 11. Assessment and plan

**Diagnosis:** Parkinson disease, clinically established by MDS 2015 criteria. Bradykinesia with rest tremor and rigidity (right-predominant); supportive criteria present: rest tremor, olfactory loss and a clear response to levodopa (37.5% on challenge); no absolute exclusion criteria; no red flags. Prodromal features include REM sleep behavior disorder and constipation. MDS-UPDRS III (OFF) 32, Hoehn and Yahr 2.

**Differential diagnosis and how each was addressed:**
- Essential tremor: rest tremor with bradykinesia, rigidity, hyposmia, and an asymmetric DAT-SPECT deficit; excluded.
- Drug-induced parkinsonism: no dopamine-blocking drugs, and abnormal DAT-SPECT; excluded.
- Vascular parkinsonism: MRI shows only punctate white matter foci (Fazekas 1), no infarcts, and gait is not lower-body predominant; excluded.
- Multiple system atrophy: no orthostatic hypotension, no urinary failure, no cerebellar signs, normal pons and putamen on MRI; considered unlikely.
- Progressive supranuclear palsy: no vertical gaze palsy, no early falls, no midbrain atrophy; excluded.
- Dementia with Lewy bodies: cognition intact (MoCA 27/30) and motor onset well before any cognitive change; considered unlikely.
- Normal-pressure hydrocephalus: no gait, urinary and cognitive triad; ventricles normal.

**Discussion with patient and wife:** We explained that Parkinson disease is a chronic, slowly progressive condition with effective symptom treatment, and that many people work and stay active for years. Options: (1) levodopa/carbidopa, the most effective, with a risk of wearing-off and dyskinesia over years; (2) a MAO-B inhibitor, milder benefit and few side effects; (3) a dopamine agonist, which we avoid because of sleepiness and impulse-control problems; (4) no medication yet. His priorities are to keep teaching for 4 more years, keep playing guitar, and avoid dyskinesia. He could explain back that levodopa is used when function is affected and that starting it does not "use up" its benefit. He chose to start treatment now.

**Plan**
1. Start levodopa/carbidopa 100/25 mg: half a tablet three times daily for 1 week, then 1 tablet three times daily; and rasagiline 1 mg once daily. Levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD) will be 300 (levodopa) + 100 (rasagiline) = 400 mg/day.
2. Referral to physiotherapy (LSVT BIG / PD-specific exercise) and speech-language pathology (LSVT LOUD) for hypophonia.
3. Constipation: fibre and polyethylene glycol; review REM sleep behavior disorder, bed safety measures and melatonin if needed.
4. Non-motor review at each visit (orthostatic blood pressure, mood, cognition, sleep, impulse control).
5. Follow-up in 3 months, then every 6 months.

**Dr. K. Ramanathan**, Neurology, Movement Disorders (fictitious)

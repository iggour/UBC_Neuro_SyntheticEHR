## 2. History of Present Illness

**Onset pattern:** Insidious, progressive.

**Symptom duration:** Approximately 22 months from first symptom to current review; approximately 7 months from first neurological consultation to initiation of symptomatic treatment.

The patient's symptoms began in **November 2024**, when his spouse first noted involuntary, irregular, non-purposeful movements of the fingers and distal upper limbs. At that time the patient attributed the movements to stress and fatigue, and no medical attention was sought.

By **June 2025**, the chorea had spread to involve the shoulders, trunk, and lower limbs, and the patient reported mild imbalance on stairs. His family also noted increasing irritability and reduced motivation, although he continued to work full-time.

On **2026-02-09**, his family physician documented generalized choreiform movements and referred him to neurology.

At his **first neurology consultation on 2026-03-16**, examination demonstrated generalized chorea. Baseline severity scoring showed a UHDRS Total Motor Score of 34/124, MoCA 25/30, SDMT 38, and letter fluency (F) 11 words/minute. A brain MRI was ordered, and genetic testing for an HTT CAG repeat expansion was ordered after a structured family history was obtained.

**Brain MRI on 2026-04-02** demonstrated bilateral caudate and putaminal atrophy with frontal-predominant cortical volume loss, without focal lesion or abnormal enhancement.

**HTT genetic testing returned on 2026-04-21**, showing 44 and 19 CAG repeats, confirming the diagnosis of Huntington disease.

A **genetic counseling session was completed on 2026-05-05**, during which inheritance, the 50% risk to each child, and reproductive options were discussed. Two adult siblings declined predictive testing; no testing was performed on any minor children.

**Baseline assessment on 2026-05-26** showed PHQ-9 = 6 (mild) and C-SSRS = 0 (no suicidal ideation). Baseline laboratory panel and ECG were within normal limits.

At the **multidisciplinary HD clinic review on 2026-06-09** (neurology, psychiatry, physiotherapy, speech-language pathology, social work), tetrabenazine was initiated for functionally impairing chorea after shared decision-making.

Tetrabenazine was titrated over four weeks:
- **Week 1 (2026-06-09):** 12.5 mg once daily in the morning. Tolerated, no sedation, no mood change (PHQ-9 = 6, C-SSRS = 0 on 2026-06-16).
- **Week 2 (2026-06-16):** increased to 25 mg/day divided twice daily. Chorea subjectively reduced.
- **Week 3 (2026-06-23):** increased to 37.5 mg/day divided three times daily.
- **Week 4 (2026-06-30):** increased to 50 mg/day divided three times daily. UHDRS Total Motor Score improved to 27/124 on 2026-07-07.

At **follow-up on 2026-07-21**, chorea had further improved on 50 mg/day. No dose escalation beyond 50 mg/day was planned without CYP2D6 genotyping.

**CYP2D6 genotyping was performed on 2026-08-18** because of residual chorea and consideration of exceeding 50 mg/day. The result on **2026-09-01** was *1/*1, extensive metabolizer. Tetrabenazine was therefore increased to 62.5 mg/day divided three times daily (25 mg, 25 mg, 12.5 mg).

At the **current review on 2026-09-29**, chorea is well controlled on tetrabenazine 62.5 mg/day. PHQ-9 = 5, C-SSRS = 0. There has been no dysphagia and no falls in the past 3 months.
